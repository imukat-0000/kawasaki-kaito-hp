Kinshu

KINSHU

Graduation Exhibition ‘Kinshu’74th Tokyo University of the Arts Graduation & Diploma Works Exhibition (2026)
Salon de Printemps Award

Each metal keeps its own colour, drawn out of different copper alloys and metalworking techniques. Layered together, those colours weave a single landscape.

Likened to brocade woven from coloured threads, the colours of autumn — the season of ripening — fill the room: an exhibition marking the close of graduate study.

Composition

COMPOSITION
  • The wave‘Autumn Leaves’ — across walls, plinths and floor, weighted to the right. Falling leaves were fixed to the walls and the sides of the plinths.
  • Mount Fuji‘Nishikigoi’ — placed at the lower left of the centre of the room.
  • The boats‘Vermilion Wakin Cluster’ — weighted to the left, opposite the leaves.

The overall layout follows the composition of Katsushika Hokusai’s The Great Wave off Kanagawa, from Thirty-six Views of Mount Fuji. Most works stand on plinths to gain height.

Statement

STATEMENT

One account of the origin of the Japanese word for autumn, aki, traces it to akiru — to be filled, to be satisfied, as plants bear fruit.

Autumn, the season of ripening, is also a turning point: one cycle reaches fullness and gives way to the next season.

On completing graduate school, the culmination of the knowledge and skills built up so far, and the turning point of stepping beyond them, are laid over the scenery of autumn.

The work uses a range of copper alloys and metalworking techniques to draw out the colours inherent in each material, and the many colours and patterns born of surface treatment.

The way the colours of different metals overlap and weave a single landscape is likened to brocade woven from coloured threads, expressing the richness of autumn.

What has been learned bears fruit as a single form, and in time leads on to new expression.

‘Kinshu’ carries thoughts on the path so far, and a wish for the season to come.

Kaito Kawasaki (English translation)
